# Makers' generators and generated meshes

Two ways to start a design without describing it from scratch. Both end in a real, editable design: parameters with
sliders, the full code, the checks, the slice for a print design. Neither one hands over a baked STL.

## Makers' generators

One click on Home (under the bar), one MCP tool each, or `form make <generator>` in a terminal. Each is a parametric
design with good defaults that is built in the kernel before it is saved and passes the print checks at its presets
(`tests/test_makers.py` runs every one through geometry, exports and checks; no check may fail).

| Generator | Presets | What it is | Tool |
|---|---|---|---|
| `name_tag` | keychain, name_tag, engraved | Text in real font outlines (Form Studio's bundled Instrument Sans 700, SIL OFL 1.1), raised or engraved on a rounded plate that grows with it, with a keyring hole | `make_name_tag` |
| `lithophane` | lithophane, relief | A picture as a height field on a frame: thick where dark (to hold to the light) or high where bright. The picture is reduced to a grid of grey levels in the design (file name and SHA-256 kept, the picture itself not); the surface is a mesh body, exact at every sample, so it exports STL and 3MF and never STEP | `make_lithophane` |
| `gridfinity` | bin, tray, parts_tray, baseplate | The built-in Gridfinity recipes with tray and parts-tray presets | `make_gridfinity` |
| `knob` | knurled, smooth, large | D-shaft knob: skirt, diamond knurl or smooth band, dished cap, pointer groove, M3 set-screw pilot | `make_knob` |
| `cable_clip` | single, double, thick | One to four snap-in rings on a strip with countersunk screw holes, one extruded profile, printed flat | `make_cable_clip` |
| `hook` | coat, small, heavy | J hook on a back plate with two countersunk holes, printed on its side so the layers run along the arm | `make_hook` |
| `stand` | phone, tablet | The `device_stand` recipe sized for a phone or a tablet | `make_stand` |
| `drawer_divider` | cutlery, desk, small | An insert sized to a drawer, divided into rounded compartments | `make_drawer_divider` |
| `hinge_box` | box, slim, deep | Box and lid printed open in one piece with a print-in-place pin hinge and a snap latch. The hinge clearance comes from the fit model: 0.35 mm per side, moved by the printer's measured bias when it is calibrated, recorded with its provenance | `make_hinge_box` |
| `threaded_jar` | printing, iso, spice | Jar and screw lid side by side: a 45-degree printing thread at any pitch, or ISO metric (ISO 68-1 profile, ISO 261 coarse pitch of the nearest size) | `make_threaded_jar` |
| `spur_gears` | 1:2, 1:3, 1:1 | The gear calculator's spur pair (ISO 21771 geometry, Lewis bending); every revision runs the calculation again | `make_spur_gears` |

```
form make list
form make name_tag keychain --text "MAYA" cap_h=9
form make lithophane --image photo.jpg width=120
form make hinge_box inner_w=90
form make threaded_jar iso
```

Source generators write their design from a template in `form/makers/templates/`; the template is itself a working
design, and what the generator was given (the text, the picture grid) is in its `MAKE` dict. Every parameter can be
changed afterwards like any other design's.

What the checks say, as they are: a lithophane's thinnest part is its thinnest setting (0.8 mm by default), which the
wall check reports as a warning; a thread's lower flanks are 45 degrees (printing profile) or 60 degrees (ISO) from
vertical, which the overhang check may report.

## Image or words to a mesh, then to CAD

`generate_mesh` (MCP), `form generate run <provider> --text ... | --image ...`, or **Generate from words or a
picture** on Home. A mesh generator makes a mesh; Form Studio then:

1. scales it so its longest side is `size_mm` (a generated mesh has no real size, so the size is assumed and the design
   says so);
2. splits it into parts at its concave creases (`form/recon/partseg.py`: an edge is a cut where the surface folds
   inward by more than 28 degrees; regions under 4 % of the area are merged into a neighbour), closing each part;
3. triages each part (mechanical, organic or mixed) and rebuilds them as one design: one part by the route its triage
   chooses (parametric, surface body or mesh body); several parts by the parametric reconstruction of each, which keeps
   a part it cannot make parametric (typically an organic one) as its verified Layer 1 surfaces or Layer 0 solid;
4. keeps each part's deviation map and two-sided distance, and labels the design **generated by
   &lt;provider&gt;/&lt;model&gt;** (`generated` in the project, its brief, the activity log).

The mesh stays a reference; it is never the design.

### Generators

There is no default. With none set up, Form Studio says so.

| Generator | Where it runs | Needs | Licence |
|---|---|---|---|
| TRELLIS.2 (`trellis2`) | This computer, in its own environment | Installed by you from github.com/microsoft/TRELLIS.2 (its `setup.sh`). Its README: Linux, an NVIDIA GPU with at least 24 GB, CUDA Toolkit 12.4. Picture to 3D only. `form generate trellis2 <folder> [--python PY]` checks the folder's LICENSE is MIT and records it with its SHA-256 | MIT, code and weights (README) |
| Meshy (`meshy`) | api.meshy.ai | Your Meshy API key (`meshy_api_key`, Settings > Agents) and the generator turned on | Meshy's terms |
| Tripo (`tripo`) | openapi.tripo3d.ai | Your Tripo API key (`tripo_api_key`) and the generator turned on | Tripo's terms |
| Hunyuan 3D (`hunyuan`) | — | Not wired: the hosted API is not implemented. Its community licence does not apply in the EU, the UK and South Korea | Tencent Hunyuan 3D 2.1 Community License |

A hosted generator needs both your key and your switch (`form generate enable meshy`, or Settings). Before every run
Form Studio states what is sent (the description, or the picture with its size and SHA-256), to which host, what
comes back, and that it is billed to your account; the run carries the consent token of that statement. In the app
that is the dialog's **Send it**; in a terminal, the question (or `--yes`); over MCP, a confirmation card in Form
Studio's chat or your client's own form. Without your yes nothing is sent. Turning a generator on and storing a key
are never available to an agent's tools.

Tests use a fixture provider that returns a known mesh; no test reaches a hosted API (`tests/test_generate.py`
replaces the network call with one that fails the test).
