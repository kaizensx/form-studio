How we measure

Every number on this site comes from a run you can repeat. Three benches live in the repository; each is a script that builds real designs with the engine, measures the result and writes its own report. The figures below are from the runs of 25 November 2026.

Mesh to editable CAD

Seven synthetic parts are built with build123d, exported as STL and handed to Make editable, plus three copies of one part with scan artifacts (vertex noise, quantised coordinates, missing triangles). The result is compared with the mesh it came from. B-rep is the share of the surface that became real CAD faces instead of a facet copy. Editable is the share of the parameters that are real dimensions: each one is moved 0.5 mm and the part must still build. Deviation is the two-sided Hausdorff distance to the mesh.

All ten results are within tolerance (0.02 mm for the clean parts, 0.05 mm for the scan artifacts) and pass the occupancy, void and opening gates. The parts are synthetic and mostly prismatic or turned; an organic scan keeps more of its surface as an exact facet copy. The full report has every column, and scripts/recon_bench.py writes it.

Automatic design refinement

Twenty-two creation briefs, from a shaft coupler to a helmet in four parts, are run twice through the MCP server by a scripted agent (deterministic, no model): once with refinement on, once with it off. The critic is the design bench's score out of 100.

Refinement kept 30 changes across 21 of the 22 designs: material zones, a fillet hierarchy, an edge break. It runs in the background after the build, so the first shape arrives as fast as without it. Sixteen briefs score the same on and off: material zones change what the photo shows, not the critic. The full report lists every brief; scripts/creation_bench.py writes it.

Game assets Beta

Six original game-asset briefs and one deliberately plain control go the whole way: a model, levels of detail, UVs, baked textures and a GLB that is read back and validated.

Technical validation passes on six of six, and every level of detail is inside its triangle budget. The visual rubric is a harder test: three blind passes by an AI model over anonymous review sheets, with the control mixed in. The assets score 2.8 to 3.3 of 5 against a gate of 4.0, so the gate is not met yet; the control scores 0.62, as it must. The sixth asset, a remote control built as CAD first, is in the full report, written by scripts/game_bench.py.

Measured on every change

Two more pages are kept by the project's automation. Build timings records how long each recipe takes to build, check and slice and how fast the app starts, on every change to the development branch. Slicer compatibility runs each supported slicer for real and shows which versions slice the sample designs.
